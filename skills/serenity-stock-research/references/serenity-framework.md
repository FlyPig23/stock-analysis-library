## Core Idea: The Shiso Leaf

The visible winner is often not the best asymmetry. In the user's analogy, everyone watches the premium tuna, but the restaurant cannot operate without the shiso leaf. In AI hardware, the visible winners are NVIDIA, Microsoft, hyperscalers, and large foundries. The shiso leaf may be a substrate, laser, external light source, specialty glass, test tool, power component, or obscure material supplier.

The research task is to find:

1. a demand wave that is already locked in,
2. a dependency layer the market has not modeled,
3. a supplier base of one or two real volume players,
4. a company small enough that the new demand is material,
5. evidence that the bottleneck is moving from theory to revenue.

## Serenity Method in Three Moves

### 1. Drill Down From End Demand

Start with an obvious demand wave such as AI GPU clusters, CPO, AI agents, data-center power, or robotics. Then keep asking what each layer requires.

Example chains:

- AI compute -> data centers -> optical interconnect -> CPO -> external light source -> CW lasers -> qualified suppliers.
- AI optical modules -> laser chips -> InP/GaAs substrates -> boule growth and wafer processing -> supplier concentration.
- Agentic AI hardware -> developer adoption -> cheap edge devices -> Raspberry Pi units -> revenue estimate revisions.
- AI data-center power -> 800V DC architecture -> SiC/power semis -> specialty foundry or power-module supplier.
- AI data-center power -> fabless/fab-lite GaN/SiC designers -> merchant foundry capacity -> Western high-volume qualified fabs.

### 2. Count the Suppliers

Use supplier count as a first-pass filter:

- three or more credible suppliers: usually pass unless one has an obvious qualification or capacity advantage
- two suppliers: investigate deeply
- one supplier or practical monopoly: priority candidate

Confirm that suppliers have volume manufacturing, customer qualification, and realistic capacity. Lab capability alone is not enough.

### 3. Publish/Attack the Thesis Before Acting

The Serenity-inspired habit is adversarial: expose the chain to technical rebuttal, expert comments, and alternative-supplier claims before conviction. A good research note should invite disproof:

- “What supplier did I miss?”
- “What architecture makes this obsolete?”
- “What customer qualification is not real?”
- “What is the fastest capacity response?”
- “What data point would invalidate this?”

Do not use an LLM as the only critic. Search for opposing evidence and expert disagreement.

## Bayesian Extension: Probability Before Certainty

The user's later framing maps the Serenity method onto Bayesian reasoning. Use this as the mental model when the request asks for conviction, sizing, timing, or rotation.

### 1. Build a High-Quality Prior

Initial research is not just background reading. It defines the prior probability `P(H)` that a company is a real underpriced chokepoint.

Good priors come from physical and structural evidence:

- academic papers and technical roadmaps
- BOM and dependency maps
- supplier concentration and qualification cycles
- patent position, process know-how, yield, and capacity
- customer validation, long-term agreements, and procurement behavior

Weak priors come from price momentum, headlines, simple revenue extrapolation, or secondhand influencer claims.

The core advantage is that the prior is anchored in the physical reality of the supply chain, not in the market's current narrative.

### 2. Act Under Incomplete Information

The method does not require 100% certainty. Once the research implies the market is assigning too low a probability to the chokepoint hypothesis, the investment decision is a Bayesian expected-value bet.

Frame the decision as:

`expected value = posterior probability of bottleneck outcome * upside if true - probability of failure * downside if false`

Use action labels instead of overclaiming:

- small speculative candidate: high asymmetry but still low evidence
- core candidate: high structural prior plus confirming evidence
- wait for pullback: strong thesis but poor price/position comfort
- watch only: interesting prior but missing proof
- avoid: weak prior or negative updates dominate

### 3. Update Conviction With Evidence

During research or holding, every new fact updates the hypothesis:

`H = this company is a future key bottleneck`

Strong positive evidence increases posterior conviction:

- exclusive or hard-to-copy patent/process advantage
- named customer qualification or long-term supply agreement
- backlog, pricing power, allocation language, or margin inflection
- capacity expansion funded by customers or strategic programs
- independent technical validation from experts or standards bodies

Strong negative evidence reduces posterior conviction:

- credible substitute technology or architecture shift
- hidden supplier with comparable capacity
- key customer internalizing supply
- repeated dilution before revenue inflection
- weak management integrity, accounting, or execution
- evidence that qualification does not convert into production revenue

The question is not "did the stock rise?" The question is "is the original bottleneck hypothesis being strengthened or weakened by facts?"

### 4. Use Sequential Updating and Opportunity Cost

Treat the market as a dynamic probability field. The posterior from one research cycle becomes the prior for the next cycle.

Compare opportunities across layers:

- Is the current holding still the most active bottleneck?
- Has the market already repriced the old thesis?
- Did new evidence move another supply-chain layer from possible to probable?
- Is expected value better in a lower-position candidate with stronger evidence momentum?

Portfolio rotation should follow changes in probability-weighted opportunity, not short-term price envy.

### Bayesian Research Log Template

Use this compact table in reports when conviction or portfolio action matters:

| Item | What to Record |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis | The exact chokepoint claim being tested |
| Prior | Why the structure made the claim plausible before confirmation |
| Market Mispricing | What consensus appears to be underestimating |
| Positive Evidence | Facts that are more likely if the hypothesis is true |
| Negative Evidence | Facts that are more likely if the hypothesis is false |
| Posterior Direction | Up, flat, down, or invalidated |
| Action Label | Core candidate, speculative, wait, watch, avoid |
| Next Evidence | The concrete data point that would update the thesis again |

## XFAB-Style Pattern: Policy-Backed Replacement Value

This pattern applies when the company is a fab, foundry, specialty manufacturer, or strategic upstream supplier whose near-term financials are partly hidden by legacy cyclicality.

### 1. Start From Architecture, Then Trace Commercial Routing

Begin with the architecture push, not the ticker:

`NVIDIA/platform requirement -> new subsystem need -> fabless/fab-lite beneficiaries -> who manufactures for them -> qualified horizontal supplier`

The key move is to prefer the horizontal bottleneck. A vertically integrated incumbent may benefit, but a merchant foundry or qualified supplier can capture demand from multiple winners.

### 2. Add the Western Supply-Chain Premium

For strategic semiconductors, photonics, MEMS, power devices, advanced packaging, and materials, check whether the bottleneck is scarce in the U.S. or Europe even if Asia has broader capacity. Western capability can carry a premium when customers and governments want non-China or local supply chains.

Use government action as validation:

- CHIPS Act grants, PMTs, state subsidies, and EU programs
- national-security or strategic-supply-chain wording
- inclusion in official ecosystem blueprints
- funded capex tied to high-volume manufacturing

Do not count grants as revenue. Treat them as evidence that governments may backstop capex, accelerate qualification, and steer customers toward the supplier.

### 3. Look for Masked Segment Inflection

Blended revenue can hide the relevant signal. Separate:

- high-growth segment: SiC, GaN, silicon photonics, MEMS, CPO-related capacity
- legacy drag: automotive downturn, mature-node weakness, old SOI cycles, inventory digestion

The Serenity-style question is whether the market is pricing the whole company as a depressed legacy asset while a small strategic segment is inflecting.

### 4. Use Replacement Value, Not Only P/E

For asset-heavy manufacturers, P/B is only the starting point. Estimate what it would cost and how long it would take to recreate equivalent qualified capacity:

- land, fabs, tools, cleanrooms, process recipes, yield history
- customer qualifications and reliability data
- funded capex and subsidies
- whether accounting book understates or overstates the true asset value

The attractive setup is: market cap near or below realistic replacement value, grants reduce future funding risk, and the operating business is treated as free optionality.

### 5. Bridge and Optionality Stack

Prefer names with a near-term bridge plus long-term option:

- bridge: current SiC, GaN, MEMS, or specialty foundry revenue that can grow before the big theme matures
- option: silicon photonics, CPO, advanced power architecture, or another later-cycle demand wave
- catalyst: CHIPS awards, qualification announcements, customer revenue commitments, segment growth disclosure

This is usually a rerating thesis, not necessarily a 10x thesis. A plausible outcome may be 2x-4x if the market moves from liquidation/legacy valuation toward strategic-asset valuation.

### 6. Failure Modes

Reject or downgrade if:

- the strategic segment never converts from qualification to production revenue
- grants fund capex but shareholders absorb dilution and low returns
- replacement-value math relies on obsolete or unprofitable assets
- Asian capacity destroys pricing before Western premium appears
- one named partner is used to imply revenue without disclosed production commitment

## Case Templates

### AXTI / InP Substrate

Pattern:

- visible wave: AI data-center optical interconnect
- hidden layer: InP/GaAs substrate capacity
- chokepoint: few volume suppliers, long expansion cycle
- evidence to verify: revenue mix, customer signals, capacity, raw material access, competitor capacity, gross margin
- key risk: commodity-like oversupply, alternative materials, China exposure, valuation after rerating

### SIVE / CW Laser for CPO

Pattern:

- visible wave: CPO and 1.6T/3.2T optical links
- hidden layer: external light source because silicon does not emit light efficiently
- chokepoint: qualified CW laser supplier and IP holder
- evidence to verify: named design wins, Jabil/Marvell/Celestial/Ayar/Lightmatter/POET references, bookings, revenue pipeline, EU/US CHIPS support
- key risk: qualification without volume revenue, competing laser suppliers, architecture delay, dilution, high short interest/reflexivity

### RPI / Developer-Demand Estimate Miss

Pattern:

- visible wave: AI agents and cheap edge deployment
- hidden signal: developer community and product hoarding before analyst model updates
- evidence to verify: GitHub repositories, forums, product availability, distributor data, company guidance, sell-through
- key risk: one-time hoarding, meme-stock reflexivity, channel inventory, overestimating revenue materiality

### XFAB / Policy-Backed Merchant Foundry

Pattern:

- visible wave: NVIDIA 800V DC data-center power, Western semiconductor reshoring, silicon photonics/CPO
- hidden layer: fabless and fab-lite power-semi players still need qualified high-volume foundry capacity
- chokepoint: horizontal Western SiC/GaN/MEMS/silicon-photonics foundry capacity, especially where vertically integrated players do not offer merchant access
- evidence to verify: U.S./EU grants, CHIPS Act language, Department of Commerce or EU validation, named customer/partner qualifications, segment revenue growth, funded capex, replacement-value math
- key risk: automotive/legacy drag persists, qualification does not become volume revenue, subsidies do not prevent dilution, China/Asia capacity compresses pricing, replacement value is overstated

## Report Scoring Rubric

Score from 1 to 5:

- demand certainty
- chokepoint strength
- supplier concentration
- evidence quality
- valuation/position comfort
- liquidity and tradability
- management/financial quality
- catalyst clarity
- downside/invalidation visibility

High-conviction research requires high demand/chokepoint/evidence scores and at least acceptable liquidity and downside clarity. A low 52-week position alone does not create conviction.

## Red Flags

- The company is mentioned only once casually.
- The thesis depends only on TAM charts.
- The company has no current production or no named customer.
- Government grants are treated as revenue.
- The ticker is OTC/illiquid and moved immediately after influencer posts.
- The influencer's entry and exit are unknown.
- The thesis assumes one technology route wins permanently.
- The company must dilute before the revenue inflection arrives.
- The stock is already near a 52-week high and the next catalyst is vague.
