# Frank-Inspired Capital Rotation Framework

This reference distills the supplied qinbafrank interview into reusable research rules. Treat it as a framework, not as a source of current facts. Verify all market data, dates, IPO terms, company metrics, and macro claims before using them in an answer.

## Core Premise

In a limited-liquidity environment, the market is usually not a broad bull market. Capital concentrates in assets with either:

- real earnings validation
- credible forward expectations
- scarcity that creates pricing power
- architecture upgrades that force spending
- far-future optionality with believable milestones

The central question is not "what is hot?" but:

`Which lane has improving proof while the prior lane is becoming crowded, fully priced, or vulnerable to a reset?`

## Market Regime Checklist

Classify the environment before naming winners:

- Liquidity: policy rate path, Fed balance sheet, TGA/RRP, credit spreads, dollar, long yields.
- Macro shocks: CPI, energy/oil, geopolitics, recession indicators, carry-trade pressure, funding stress.
- Breadth: equal-weight vs cap-weight indices, sector participation, new highs/lows, high-beta performance.
- AI commercialization: hyperscaler cloud growth, capex guidance, model-company revenue/ARR, token usage, enterprise adoption, pricing tiers, ARPU.
- Flow events: mega IPOs, lockups, index inclusion, ETF rebalancing, secondary offerings, buybacks, large issuance.

Regime labels:

- `broad liquidity bull`: many assets rise together because liquidity is abundant.
- `structured bull`: only themes with proof or narrative strength attract durable capital.
- `valuation reset`: prices got ahead of proof.
- `earnings reset`: growth/guidance misses expectations.
- `thesis reset`: the strategic logic is damaged.
- `macro-logic reset`: the market's base macro assumption breaks.

## Three AI Rotation Logics

### 1. Scarcity Logic

Capital rotates into assets where demand exposes a shortage. Evidence should be concrete:

- price increases
- allocation language
- backlog or book-to-bill improvement
- lead-time extension
- capacity sold out
- customer urgency
- margin expansion from supply tightness

Typical AI examples:

- GPU capacity and accelerators
- HBM, DRAM, GDDR, NAND, SSDs, HDDs, hot/cold storage
- CPU demand for agent orchestration and scheduling
- power/cooling components if demand exceeds buildout capacity

Failure modes:

- shortage is only inventory restocking
- competitors add capacity quickly
- customer architecture shifts away
- margins do not follow tightness
- valuation already discounts peak shortage

### 2. Upgrade Logic

Capital rotates into assets required by a new architecture or efficiency standard. Evidence can start as roadmap proof, but must eventually convert into customer adoption.

Typical AI examples:

- optical interconnect upgrades: 800G -> 1.6T -> 3.2T, LPO/NPO/CPO
- lasers, optical engines, DSPs, test, packaging, substrates, reliability tooling
- datacenter power upgrade: 48V/54V -> 800V HVDC and related power management
- advanced packaging, 3D stacking, glass/ceramic substrates, test and inspection equipment

Failure modes:

- technology is real but adoption is delayed
- reliability or serviceability blocks deployment
- hyperscalers choose a cheaper bridge technology
- revenue timing is too far away for current valuation
- a component is replaceable or margin-poor despite being in the stack

### 3. Far-Future Logic

Capital rotates into option value before earnings are visible. This can work, but position sizing and evidence standards must be stricter.

Typical AI examples:

- edge AI and on-device AI
- Physical AI, robotics, autonomous driving
- AI-RAN and edge infrastructure
- industrial, medical, automotive, or security software tied to physical systems

Required milestones:

- named platform/customer relationship
- pilot to production evidence
- regulatory/security certification where relevant
- unit economics or cost-down path
- adoption catalyst such as robot/vehicle/edge-node volume ramp

Failure modes:

- story stays optional and never converts
- the business is too small inside the company to move numbers
- capital chases a loose association with AI
- legacy business weakness overwhelms the option

## Pullback Diagnosis

Use three single-stock pullback labels:

- `valuation kill`: the company is still executing, but price/valuation outran evidence.
- `earnings kill`: reported growth, guide, margins, or order timing missed expectations.
- `thesis kill`: the core reason to own it is invalidated, such as losing a strategic customer, being designed out, or seeing the architecture move elsewhere.

Use three market correction labels:

- `small`: single-digit or mild sector correction, often valuation plus macro noise.
- `medium`: roughly mid-teens index/sector correction, usually macro event plus growth worry.
- `large`: 25%+ style reset that changes the macro or commercialization premise.

For AI equities, the key dividing line is whether AI commercialization is still accelerating. If hyperscaler cloud growth, model revenue, capex confidence, and usage are intact, many corrections are valuation or medium-cycle resets. If those data break, the rotation map must be rebuilt from the base layer.

## Software Rotation Filter

Classify software into:

- Vulnerable: generic workflows an agent/model can perform without a third-party tool.
- Reinforced: vertical software with private data, domain know-how, workflow depth, or regulated/customer-specific context.
- Physical moat: software tied to hardware, network infrastructure, security, CDN, industrial systems, or device fleets.

When software sells off, ask whether funds are leaving because of AI replacement risk, temporary capex/interest-rate pressure, or a true thesis break.

## Rotation Map Template

Use this table for medium/full answers:

| Lane | Logic | Stage | Evidence | Crowding | Next Confirmation | Invalidation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HBM/storage | scarcity | earnings validation | pricing, backlog, margins | high/medium/low | next earnings and capex | pricing rollover |
| Optical interconnect | upgrade | roadmap to ramp | customer adoption, speed transition | high/medium/low | volume deployment | reliability delay |
| Edge/Physical AI | far-future | option | named partners, pilots | high/medium/low | production ramp | no revenue conversion |

Add tickers only after the lane is defined.

## Rotation Verdict Labels

- `early accumulation research`: proof improving, crowding still low.
- `confirmed rotation`: price action and fundamentals both support the lane.
- `crowded but valid`: thesis intact, but valuation/pullback risk is high.
- `wait for pullback`: right lane, poor entry setup.
- `watch only`: story is interesting but proof is weak.
- `rotate out / de-risk`: original lane has become fully priced or evidence is weakening.
- `avoid until thesis repaired`: thesis damage requires new proof.

## Evidence Hierarchy

Strongest:

- filings, earnings calls, investor presentations
- official customer/supplier announcements
- index/exchange notices, central-bank/government data

Strong:

- capacity expansion notices
- price increase disclosures
- backlog/order-book data
- named design wins or production ramps

Medium:

- reputable industry reporting
- broker or market-research summaries
- conference notes with verifiable company statements

Weak:

- social posts, unsourced screenshots, rumor threads, forum claims

Use weak evidence only as a lead to verify, not as a conclusion.

## Default Output Skeleton

1. `Verdict`: one sentence on where capital is rotating and why.
2. `Regime`: liquidity/macro/business-validation context.
3. `Rotation Map`: source -> destination lanes.
4. `Lane Ranking`: scarcity, upgrade, far-future buckets with evidence and risks.
5. `Ticker Roles`: optional, grouped by role and evidence maturity.
6. `Pullback/Exit Triggers`: valuation, earnings, thesis, macro.
7. `Next Checks`: earnings dates, CPI/Fed data, product ramps, IPO/index events.
8. `Disclaimer`: research only, not financial advice.
